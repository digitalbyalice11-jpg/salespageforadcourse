Breaking Down
Each Campaign

Tap each one to go deeper. I'm going to explain what it actually does, when to use it, and what I personally do — so you're not guessing.

Here's the thing nobody tells you when you start: you cannot sell to strangers. People don't buy from accounts they've never seen before. The awareness campaign fixes that — it gets your face, your offer, and your energy in front of new people before you ever ask them to spend a dollar.

What it does: It tells Meta "show this to as many relevant people as possible." You'll pay less per impression because you're not asking the algorithm to find buyers yet — just eyeballs. Think of it as the first hello.

What to actually run: Don't stress about making new content for this. Go look at your analytics and find the reels that performed best organically — the ones people actually watched and saved. Put a small daily budget behind those. If it worked without money, it'll work harder with it.

When you need it: Always — but especially when you're new, just launched something, or your sales have gone quiet. Quiet sales usually means your pipeline dried up. Awareness fills it back up.

The AMCREATIVE take: Buyer certainty is built through repetition. The more someone sees you consistently showing up with value, the less scary it feels to buy from you. Awareness isn't a vanity campaign — it's the reason your sales campaign works.

Someone not buying the first time they see your offer isn't a no — it's a not yet. Life happened. They got distracted. The baby started crying. Their phone died. The traffic campaign is specifically designed to go back and find those people and bring them back to your offer.

Who goes in this audience: Anyone who's already shown interest but didn't convert. People who watched your reels past the halfway point. People who clicked through to your sales page. People who engaged with your profile recently. These people already know who you are — you're not starting from scratch with them.

What your ad should feel like: Less of a pitch, more of a reminder. You don't need to re-introduce yourself or over-explain the offer. Something simple that says "hey, you were looking at this — it's still here" is enough. Soft and direct beats loud and salesy every time for this audience.

The AMCREATIVE take: Most digital product sales don't happen on the first touchpoint. The traffic campaign is your automated follow-up — it works in the background while you're asleep, consistently bringing warm people back to your offer without you having to chase anyone.

This is the campaign that closes. It costs the most to run — and that's on purpose. Meta charges more here because it's doing the hardest job: finding people with real purchase intent, not just people who might be interested. When it's set up right, this is where your sales notifications come from.

Run it two ways at the same time:

Broad (open targeting): No interest filters, no demographic restrictions. Just hand Meta your creative and let it go find buyers. I know that sounds scary but trust me — the algorithm has data on buying behaviour that no amount of manual interest stacking can compete with. This consistently performs better than over-targeted campaigns for digital products.

Retargeting (warm audience): Point this version at the people already in your world — anyone who visited your sales page, clicked your store link, or watched a big chunk of your top reels. These are your hottest leads. They need one more reason to say yes, not a whole new introduction.

The AMCREATIVE take: If your sales campaign isn't converting, the problem is almost never the targeting. It's almost always the offer or the messaging. This campaign is basically a live test of whether your positioning is clear. If it's not working, that's data — go back and tighten up what you're saying before you touch the budget.

Know Before You Use

Advantage+ Shopping Campaigns (ASC) are Meta's fully automated campaign format. You hand it your creative, your budget, and your conversion goal — and it controls everything else: audience, placements, bidding, and creative testing. Meta positions this as the future of advertising. It's worth understanding honestly before you decide whether to use it.

What it does well: When your pixel already has 50+ purchase events and your offer is proven to convert, ASC can scale faster than any manual setup. It removes the friction of day-to-day optimisation and can find buyers in audience pockets you'd never have thought to target manually. For accounts with real data and real budgets ($50+/day), it's a legitimate accelerator.

What it does poorly: ASC is not a testing tool — it's a scaling tool. The moment you hand control to ASC, you lose the ability to isolate variables and learn what's actually working. It optimises everything simultaneously, which means when something improves (or tanks), you can't identify the cause. For anyone building a creative testing system, that's a serious problem. You're paying for efficiency, not understanding.

The real issue for beginners: ASC performs unpredictably on new accounts with limited pixel data. Below $50/day, the AI doesn't have enough spend to explore effectively — so it often performs worse than a well-structured manual campaign. Starting with ASC before your pixel has real purchase signals is one of the most common ways beginners burn budget without learning anything.

The AMCREATIVE take: Build your understanding with manual campaigns first. Test with structure. Learn what your audience responds to, which hooks work, which angles convert. Once you have a winning creative and a pixel with real data behind it, ASC becomes a useful tool to scale what's already proven. But handing the wheel to automation before you understand the road is not a strategy — it's optimism with a budget.

I see so many people run just a sales campaign, get frustrated that it's expensive and not converting, and give up on ads entirely. That's like trying to fill a bathtub with the drain open. Each campaign feeds the next one — without all three, you're always fighting an uphill battle.

Without an awareness campaign: Your sales campaign is trying to convert cold strangers who've never seen you before. They don't know you, they don't trust you, and they're definitely not buying. Your cost per sale goes through the roof because the algorithm has to do all the trust-building that content should have done over time.

Without a traffic campaign: You're losing money every single day. Every person who found your offer, thought about it, and got distracted is just gone — because you have nothing in place to bring them back. That's a huge percentage of your potential sales just disappearing.

Without a sales campaign: You're building an audience that never gets a real invitation to buy. Awareness and traffic are great but they need somewhere to go. The sales campaign is what turns all that attention into actual income.

When all three run together, you're showing up at every stage of how someone actually makes a buying decision. They discover you, they consider it, they come back when they're ready — and your system is there at every single step.

The Engine Behind Your Ads

Meet Andromeda —
Why the Algorithm Knows
More Than You Do

Most people run ads thinking the targeting is the strategy. Set the right interests, pick the right demographics, stack the right behaviours — and maybe it works. But the real reason broad targeting outperforms interest stacking isn't luck. It's Andromeda.

Andromeda is Meta's large-scale AI retrieval and ranking system — the engine that decides which ads get shown to which people. It processes hundreds of billions of ad-to-user signals in real time, using deep learning models to identify buyer intent at a level of nuance no manual targeting setup can come close to. When you choose broad targeting, you're not abandoning strategy. You're handing the wheel to a system that has seen more buyer behaviour than any human team ever could.

01
It Reads Buyer Intent, Not Just Interests
Andromeda doesn't just match your ad to someone who follows "digital marketing" accounts. It processes behavioural patterns — what people do right before a purchase — and finds users who are actively in a buying state, not just people who might be interested someday.
02
It Learns From Your Pixel Data in Real Time
Every purchase, every sales page visit, every checkout abandonment teaches Andromeda what your buyer looks like. The more conversion data your pixel has, the more precisely it can replicate that buyer profile at scale. This is why your campaigns improve over time — not because you got better at targeting, but because the model got smarter.
03
It Understands Your Creative Content
Andromeda doesn't just read your audience settings — it reads your ad. It uses computer vision and natural language processing to understand what your video is about, what emotion it conveys, and what type of person is most likely to respond to it. Your creative is sending targeting signals you aren't even aware of.
💡
What this means for you as a digital product seller: The more you restrict your targeting manually — capping ages, stacking interests, excluding audiences — the more you limit Andromeda's ability to find buyers you'd never have found on your own. The best creative strategy isn't to out-think the algorithm. It's to give it great creative, a clear conversion signal, and enough runway to learn. Then get out of the way.
Advantage+
Meta's AI-Native Ad Tools — What They Actually Do
Advantage+ Audience
Replaces manual audience selection with AI-driven discovery. Meta expands beyond your suggested audience to find conversion signals elsewhere. Can work well — but removes the control you need to run structured creative tests. If you're in a testing phase, manual broad targeting gives you cleaner data. Use Advantage+ Audience once you're scaling a proven creative, not while you're still learning what works.
Advantage+ Placements
Lets Meta decide where your ad shows — Feed, Reels, Stories, Audience Network, Messenger. This is the least controversial of the Advantage+ tools. Automatic placements generally work well and allow the system to find the cheapest CPMs across placements. Acceptable to use from the start, though worth monitoring if you notice your creative performing differently across placements.
Advantage+ Creative
Makes automatic enhancements to your creative — adjusting brightness, adding music, generating text overlays, or cropping for different placements. The problem: it modifies your creative without showing you exactly what changed or which version is running. When you can't see what Meta altered, you can't learn from it. Turn this off if you're running creative tests. Keeping your creative exactly as intended is more important than a marginal lift from automated tweaks.
Advantage+ Shopping
Full AI campaign automation — audience, budget, bidding, and creative testing handled end-to-end by Meta. Performs best with $50+/day budgets and 50+ pixel purchase events already recorded. Below those thresholds, it often underperforms manual campaigns. It's not a starting point — it's an endpoint for accounts that have already done the testing work and have proven creative to scale.

The algorithm isn't your obstacle. It's your most powerful media buyer — and it works for free once your creative and conversion signal are in place. The only thing slowing it down is the person who keeps touching the settings.

Creative Strategy

Hooks, Captions &
Creatives That Convert

Your targeting gets your ad in front of the right person. Your creative gets them to stop, watch, and click. Two completely different jobs — and both need to be right. One more thing worth knowing: Andromeda reads your creative and uses it as an additional targeting signal. A hook that speaks precisely to your buyer doesn't just stop the scroll — it also tells the algorithm exactly who to show it to next.

Hook Examples for Digital Product Sellers
Problem → Curiosity
I posted for 6 months and made $0. Then I changed one thing.
Names the exact pain your audience is living. Creates a curiosity gap they have to close.
Specificity Hook
How I made my first $347 from a digital product with 200 followers.
Specific numbers destroy skepticism. "$347" is real. "make money online" is noise.
Reframe Hook
You don't need more followers. You need this instead.
Challenges the belief your audience holds. Positions you as someone who knows something they don't.
POV / Scenario
POV: You wake up to a sale notification from a product you made once.
Activates the dream outcome. Your viewer can see themselves in it instantly.
Objection Flip
The reason your digital product isn't selling has nothing to do with your content.
Calls out the unspoken belief that content volume equals sales. Opens a loop the viewer needs closed — and positions you as someone with the real answer.
Contrast / Before & After
3 months ago I was deleting my app from embarrassment. Last week I hit $2,000 in a single day.
The contrast creates immediate emotional movement. The specificity of the time frame and number makes it feel real — not a highlight reel, but an actual trajectory the viewer can imagine being in.
Insider Reveal
Nobody talks about this part of selling digital products — but it's why most people quit.
Signals that what follows is information most people aren't saying out loud. The phrase "nobody talks about" positions your content as insider knowledge, not another recycled tutorial.
Identity Confirmation
If you've been posting for months with nothing to show for it — this is for you.
Speaks directly to the identity of the person you want to reach. No one who already has consistent sales feels called out by this — only the exact buyer you want. Specificity of situation creates immediate belonging.
01One ad set, one message. Don't try to speak to everyone in the same creative. The more specific your ad feels, the more the right person thinks it was made for them.
02Your hook is a pattern interrupt, not an introduction. Don't open by explaining who you are. Open with something that makes the viewer stop mid-scroll because it named exactly what they're feeling.
03The comment section is your creative brief. What are people saying under your posts? The exact phrases they use — those become your hooks. Real language converts better than polished copy.
04Your CTA should remove friction, not create it. "Learn more" is vague. "Tap to see the full system" is specific. Tell them exactly what happens when they click — and make it feel effortless.
05Authenticity scales better than production. A phone video filmed in your car that feels real will outperform a studio-shot ad that feels like an ad. People buy from people, not productions.
06Creative fatigue is silent. Your metrics look fine until suddenly they don't. Set a calendar reminder every 3 weeks to audit performance. Declining CTR before declining sales is your early warning sign.
07Specificity is your strongest trust signal. "I made money" is forgettable. "I made my first sale 11 days after setting this up with under 300 followers" is believable. The more specific, the more certain your buyer feels.
08Test the angle, not just the format. If an ad isn't converting, the problem usually isn't the video length or the font — it's the angle. Try a different emotion, a different pain point, a different before-and-after.
09Your creative is a targeting signal. Andromeda reads what's in your video and uses it to find more people like those who respond to it. A precise hook doesn't just stop the right scroll — it trains the algorithm on who your buyer actually is.
10Silence-proof your ads. Most people watch Reels and Stories with sound off. Your hook has to land visually — through on-screen text, facial expression, or movement — before a single word is heard. Design for mute first.

Cold · Awareness

This ad opens on a pain point or emotional state your buyer is already living — sometimes a question, sometimes a statement that lands so accurately they stop mid-scroll because they feel seen. You're not pitching anything yet. You're making the right person feel like you read their mind. When the hook connects, you've earned the next five seconds before you've said a single word about your offer.

When to use it: When you need to sort your cold audience — stop the right person scrolling and let everyone else keep moving. Best in Reels (first two seconds, no intro), Static (pain point as the visual headline), and Story (one bold line, full screen). Layers into the Problem Callout once someone feels seen and is ready for you to go deeper on that same pain.

The AMCREATIVE take: The hook is already in your buyer's head — your job is to say it out loud specifically enough to feel personal. Vague pain gets scrolled past. Specific pain makes people stop and wonder how you knew. The more precisely you name their situation, the less everything else has to work.

Cold → Warm · Awareness

You lead with the specific behavior or situation your buyer is living right now — no pitch, no solution teased yet. The job is to make them feel understood so completely that they already trust you before you've asked for anything. The key distinction: lead with behavior, not feelings. "You're posting every day and making zero sales" is observable and undeniable. "Are you feeling frustrated?" is too broad to make anyone feel specifically seen.

When to use it: When your audience knows their problem but doesn't know you yet. This builds a warm retargeting pool efficiently — anyone who watches all the way through cares enough to re-engage. Best in Talking Head (name it fast, then pivot), Carousel (one pain point per slide), or Reel with native-feeling audio. Layers into the Testimonial Ad once they feel understood and are ready for proof.

The AMCREATIVE take: When your buyer reads your ad and thinks "that's literally my life right now," you haven't just stopped their scroll — you've positioned yourself as someone who actually gets it. That's worth more than any headline formula, because trust built through recognition doesn't need to be manufactured.

Cold → Warm · Trust

You share the origin — why you built this, what problem you ran into personally, what you figured out along the way. The mistake almost everyone makes with this ad is ending the story on themselves. It only works when it ends on them. Your struggle earns you credibility. The payoff is showing the viewer that what you built came out of solving something real — and that same solution is now available to them.

When to use it: When you're reaching a new audience and need to earn trust before you can earn the sale. Most powerful as a Talking Head (real setting, no polish, hook on the moment things went wrong). Keep it under 60 seconds on Reels. Pairs with the Testimonial Ad — your story builds emotional credibility, a customer result proves it worked.

The AMCREATIVE take: Start with the moment things went wrong — not when things got good. The vulnerability of a hard moment is what makes the transformation feel real. People don't connect to success stories. They connect to someone who kept going when it wasn't working yet — and that's what makes them believe they can too.

Cold → Warm · Awareness

You open with one declarative statement — bold, specific, no preamble. Two versions work well: a strong contrarian opinion or a surprising specific number. Both make someone stop and think "wait, really?" A confident claim signals expertise before you've explained anything. For digital product sellers specifically, being direct is increasingly rare — which makes it stand out even more in a feed full of soft, hedging content.

When to use it: When your current ads are too gentle, or when you have a real differentiator you haven't been leading with. Best as Static (the claim fills the entire visual), Reel (say it to camera in two seconds, zero intro), or Talking Head paired with a stat you then explain. Pairs with the Process Ad — the bold claim creates the curiosity gap, the process ad answers it.

The AMCREATIVE take: The test is simple: if nobody would argue with it, it's not bold enough. "Digital marketing is changing" is not a claim. "Most people who fail at selling digital products are solving the wrong problem" is. The point isn't to be controversial — it's to be direct enough that the right buyer feels like you're speaking specifically to them instead of hedging for everyone.

Cold → Warm · Awareness

You structure the ad as a numbered list — three reasons, five signs, four things your page is missing. The format signals "quick and worth it" before anyone has read a word, because it looks like content rather than an ad. For digital product sellers, this is one of the most effective ways to build a warm retargeting pool without spending heavily on conversion campaigns — people who watch all the way through or save it are already interested, and now you know who they are.

When to use it: When you want to educate without it feeling like a pitch. Carousel (one point per slide) gets the highest engagement. Reel works when you keep it punchy. Pairs with Testimonial or Offer — once someone has saved or watched through a listicle, they're warm and ready for either proof or a direct reason to act.

The AMCREATIVE take: Odd numbers outperform even — three and five are your sweet spots. Seven feels like homework. Put the number in the hook: "5 things your digital product page is missing" is a hook. "Here's what most people get wrong about their sales page" is not. And each item should teach something specific enough that the answer isn't obvious — if they can guess it before reading it, the curiosity gap disappears.

Cold → Warm · Trust

You lead with a third-party mention — a feature, a podcast shoutout, an award, an editor's pick. The source doesn't need to be famous. What it signals is that someone outside your business looked, and agreed. That shift — from you saying you're good to someone else confirming it — fundamentally changes how the claim lands. It removes the "of course they'd say that" objection before it ever forms.

When to use it: Anytime you have any external mention, even a niche one. A relevant podcast your exact audience listens to will outperform a major publication they've never heard of. Best as Static (publication name prominent), Carousel (stack multiple mentions for cumulative credibility), or Reel ("we just got featured and I want to explain why"). Pairs with the Offer Ad — once the "but who says so?" objection is removed, a clear offer is usually all they need.

The AMCREATIVE take: The credibility signal needs to match the audience, not impress a different one. Think about what publications, podcasts, or accounts your specific buyer already follows and trusts — a mention there is worth more than a generic "as seen in" badge from somewhere bigger and less relevant to them.

Warm · Trust

You walk your warm audience through exactly what happens when they buy — step by step, nothing left vague. Most people don't fail to convert because they don't want the result. They fail to convert because the next step feels unclear and the unknown feels risky. This ad removes that friction completely by answering "what actually happens after I click" before the question becomes a reason to say no.

When to use it: When your warm audience is interested but not clicking, or when your DMs are regularly full of "how does this actually work?" — those questions are your creative brief for this ad. Best in Carousel (one step per slide, scannable), Talking Head (walk through it conversationally), or Story Series (one step per frame). Pairs with Features & Benefits — once they understand the process, showing exactly what they get at each step closes the remaining gap.

The AMCREATIVE take: Open with the outcome, not the process itself. "Here's what your first week looks like after you get access" is more compelling than "here's how it works." Lead with where they'll be, then walk them through how they get there. The job is to make the future feel clear enough that not buying starts to feel like the riskier choice.

Warm → Hot · Trust

The entire ad is built around someone else's result — a review, a video, a DM screenshot, a real buyer result. At this stage, the barrier isn't awareness — your warm audience already knows you exist. The barrier is belief. What they need is proof it worked for someone who looks and feels like them. That proof can only come from someone other than you.

When to use it: When your warm audience is engaged but not converting. Best as Talking Head (real person, real voice, real setting — most powerful format for this type), Static (one strong pull quote, clean design), or Carousel (one result per slide, stacked proof). If a customer filmed it on their phone in their own space, run it exactly like that — UGC-style testimonials currently outperform polished versions. Pairs with the Offer Ad — testimonial builds belief, offer gives them the next step.

The AMCREATIVE take: Lead with the result, not the compliment. "I made my first $800 in the third week" stops the scroll. "This was honestly the best purchase I've made" doesn't — because it doesn't say anything specific enough to be believed. The more specific the result, the more your actual buyer can place themselves in the same story and imagine the same outcome for themselves.

Warm → Hot · Trust

You show where your buyer is now versus where they'll be after — visually, narratively, or both. People don't buy digital products. They buy the version of themselves that has the result. This ad's job is to collapse the distance between where they are and where they want to be, and make that distance feel crossable right now — not someday.

When to use it: When your warm audience understands your offer but hasn't committed yet. The transformation has to feel real and specific, not aspirational and vague. Best as Static (strongest for clear contrast), Carousel (before on slide one, transformation revealed as they swipe), or Talking Head (tell the story, don't just show it). Pairs with the Offer Ad once the transformation is clearly visible.

The AMCREATIVE take: The before has to be uncomfortable enough to be honest. If you soften it to protect feelings, the after loses its weight — because the distance between the two states stops feeling meaningful. Your buyer is living the "before" right now. That recognition is what makes the transformation feel personal and the purchase feel necessary rather than optional.

Warm → Hot · Trust

You walk through what's included — but you never list a feature without pairing it immediately with the benefit it creates. Not "30 templates included." "30 templates so you never stare at a blank page wondering how to say what you mean again." The feature is the what. The benefit is the so what. At this stage your warm audience trusts you — they just need clarity on what they're actually getting. This ad answers "is it worth it" before they have to ask.

When to use it: When your warm audience is engaging but not clicking through to buy. The barrier at this point is usually clarity, not trust. Best as Carousel (one feature-benefit pair per slide), Talking Head (conversational walkthrough, under 60 seconds), or Story Series (one pair per frame). Pairs with the Offer Ad — once they know exactly what they're getting, give them a specific reason and moment to say yes.

The AMCREATIVE take: Lead with the feature your buyers ask about most. Whatever question shows up most often in your DMs — "does this include X?" "what exactly is in it?" — that's your opening. Answering the most common question in the ad removes a friction point before it ever slows them down on the sales page.

Warm → Hot · Conversion

You put your offer next to the alternative — a competitor, the DIY route, doing nothing, the more expensive option. Your buyer is already making this comparison in their head. This ad just helps them get to the conclusion faster. Done right it doesn't feel like a sales argument. It feels like someone finally being direct with them and making a complicated decision feel obvious.

When to use it: When buyers keep asking "how is this different from X" — or when your biggest competition isn't another brand but "I'll just figure it out myself." For digital products, that DIY alternative is usually free content, YouTube tutorials, or doing nothing. Best as Static (side-by-side contrast, clear winner) or Talking Head ("here are your two options" framing). Frame it as their decision, not your argument. Pairs with the Offer Ad once the choice is made clear.

The AMCREATIVE take: Your most common comparison for digital products isn't a competitor — it's free content. "I could just watch videos and piece it together" is the real alternative you're competing against. Make the DIY path feel slower, more uncertain, and more expensive in time — without being dismissive of their intelligence. Respect the comparison, then make the better path genuinely clear.

Hot · Conversion

Your most direct ad — no education, no warmup, no story. Just what they get, what it costs, and why now. This is the ad for your warmest audience: people who've been watching and considering but haven't pulled the trigger. The offer doesn't require a discount. A real deadline, a founding price, a limited bonus, or a genuine window all create the same effect — making "later" feel like a decision with a cost, rather than a safe neutral option.

When to use it: During launches, when your warm audience is large but not converting, for anyone who's visited your page more than once. The offer should be the first thing they see — not buried in caption copy. Best as Static (price visible, offer clear, nothing else competing), Talking Head (direct and confident, under 30 seconds). If someone sees this and doesn't convert, run Testimonial or Comparison again before showing it a second time.

The AMCREATIVE take: The only rule for urgency here is that it has to be real. Fake countdowns are easy to spot — and when someone catches one, you don't just lose the sale, you lose the weeks of trust you built to get there. Real scarcity doesn't need tricks. The cleaner and more honest the offer, the more certain your buyer feels about saying yes.

Format Guide — The Five Ways an Ad Shows Up

The ad type is the strategic decision. The format is the execution decision. The same message in a different format is a completely different experience for the viewer — knowing when each format is strongest is how you get more mileage from every idea you have.

Static

Growth Phase

Scaling What's
Already Working

Getting to your first consistent sales is one thing. Scaling them is a completely different discipline — and most people get it wrong by moving too fast or changing things that are already working. Here's how to grow without breaking the system that got you there.

01
Prove It First, Then Amplify

Before you increase a single dollar of spend, confirm you have a campaign that's actually converting profitably. Scaling a losing campaign doesn't fix it — it accelerates the loss. The only thing worth scaling is proof. Once you have it, increase your budget in small increments over several days, not all at once. The algorithm needs time to re-calibrate without resetting its learning.

02
Multiply Winners, Don't Just Feed Them

When an ad set is performing, the instinct is to keep pouring money into it. The smarter move is to duplicate it. Running two versions of the same winning ad set at a smaller individual budget often outperforms one version at a large budget — because you're spreading risk and giving the algorithm more surface area to optimise against.

03
Let Your Buyers Tell Andromeda Who to Find Next

Once you have a meaningful pool of actual buyers, you have something more valuable than any interest targeting: a real customer list. Build a lookalike from it and let the algorithm find people who share the same deep behavioural patterns as those who already paid. At scale, you can also hand this data to Advantage+ Shopping and let Andromeda's full retrieval system find your next thousand customers — this is one of the highest-quality scaling moves available to digital product sellers, and most never use it because they don't wait long enough to collect the data.

04
Know Your Numbers Before You Scale Anything

Scaling without knowing your cost per sale is just spending more money faster. Before you increase budget, know exactly what you're paying to acquire a customer and whether your product margin supports it. If your digital product sells for $27 and you're spending $30 to get a sale, more spend makes the problem worse — not better. Fix the unit economics first.

05
Protect What's Working From Your Own Impatience

The most common way people destroy a working campaign is by changing it too soon. A campaign that looks slow on day two might be your best performer by day seven — Andromeda needs time to exit its learning phase and stabilise. Set a review schedule and stick to it. Decisions made from day-by-day anxiety are almost always worse than decisions made from a week of clean data. Discipline in the observation phase is what separates operators from gamblers.

Metrics That Matter

What to Track &
What to Ignore

Most beginners track the wrong things — likes, reach, impressions. Those are vanity metrics. These are the numbers that tell you if your digital product system is actually working.

★Cost Per Purchase (CPP) — How much to get one digital product sale? Your most important number.
★ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) — For every $1 spent, how much came back? Above 2x on low-ticket is healthy.
◈Cost Per Click (CPC) — How much per link click? High CPC means your hook isn't stopping the scroll.
◈Click-Through Rate (CTR) — What % of viewers click? Benchmark: 1–3% is solid for cold audiences.
→Daily & Weekly Revenue — Keep a simple tracker. Patterns emerge over time that no dashboard will show.
→Spend Per Campaign — Are your three campaigns balanced? Don't accidentally kill awareness by overfunding sales.
