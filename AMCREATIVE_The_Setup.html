Module 01 of 09 · Foundation

Create Your Facebook
Page & Accounts

Before you can run a single ad, you need three things set up: a Facebook Business Page, a Meta Business Suite account, and access to Ads Manager. This module walks you through all three — step by step.

1.1 — Personal Facebook Account

Use Your Real Personal Facebook Account

Everything in Meta's advertising ecosystem is built on top of a personal Facebook account. Your Business Manager, your ad account, your Page — all of it is anchored to one personal profile. This is intentional. Meta uses your personal account to verify that a real person is behind the business.

⚠ Do not create a fake or separate account for this
A lot of beginners make this mistake — they create a new Facebook account specifically for business because they want to keep things separate or they don't want to use their "real" profile. This almost always backfires. Here's why:
01
Meta flags new accounts immediately. A freshly created account with no history, no friends, no posts, and no activity trying to run ads triggers Meta's fraud detection within days — sometimes hours. Your ad account gets restricted before you've spent a dollar.
02
Account age matters. Meta's system trusts older, established accounts. A personal profile with even 6–12 months of normal activity has far more credibility than a blank account created yesterday. The older your account, the less friction you'll face getting approved and scaling.
03
Your personal account is the master key. If Meta restricts or disables it, you lose access to everything attached to it — your Business Manager, your Page, your ad account, your pixel data, your audiences. Everything. Protect it.
04
You don't have to show your personal life. If privacy is your concern: go to your Facebook profile → Settings → Privacy → adjust who can see your posts, friends list, and personal information. You can lock down your personal profile completely while still using it to run ads. Meta only cares that you exist — not what you post.
💡 Already have an account?
You're good. Move on to 1.2. If your account is older and established, that's an advantage — don't throw it away by creating something new.
✓ No account yet?
Go to facebook.com → click Create new account → fill in your real name, email or phone, password, birthday, and gender → click Sign Up → verify your email or phone. Use your real information. Then spend a few days using the account normally before moving to ads — like a page or two, post something, let it breathe. Don't rush straight from account creation to running ads.

Once you're in, you'll land on the Business Manager dashboard. Bookmark it — you'll come back to it constantly. Running ads directly from a personal profile is risky and limits your options. Business Manager is where everything lives.

Module 02 of 09 · Security

Security & Two-Factor
Authentication

Ad accounts get hacked every single day. People lose months of work, thousands in ad spend, and their entire business presence in one attack. This module takes 10 minutes and protects everything you're about to build.

2.1 — Why This Matters

The Risk Is Real

Meta ad accounts are a target for hackers because they have payment methods attached. If someone gets into your account, they can run their own ads on your card before you even notice. Meta can take days or weeks to resolve unauthorized access — and during that time your account may be disabled entirely.

⚠ This is not optional
Two-factor authentication (2FA) is the single most important security step you can take. Do not skip this module. Do not come back to it later. Do it now, before you spend a single dollar.
2.2 — Enable 2FA on Your Personal Facebook Account

Securing Your Personal Profile First

Your personal Facebook account is the master key to everything — your Page, your Business Suite, your Ads Manager. Secure it first.

01
Go to your Facebook profile → click the menu icon (≡) → Settings & Privacy → Settings
02
Click Security and Login (on desktop) or Password and Security (on mobile)
03
Find Two-Factor Authentication → click Edit → click Get Started
04
Choose Authentication App — this is the most reliable method. Download one if you don't have it:
— Google Authenticator (free, iOS & Android)
— Microsoft Authenticator (free, iOS & Android)
— Authy (free, iOS & Android — supports cloud backup, recommended)
05
Open the authenticator app and scan the QR code Facebook shows you
06
Enter the 6-digit code the app generates to confirm it's connected
07
Save your recovery codes somewhere safe — screenshot them or write them down. These are your backup if you ever lose your phone. If you don't save them now you will regret it later

YouTube search"how to set up two factor authentication Facebook 2026" — look for a short walkthrough that shows the exact settings screens

Securing Your Business Account

01

2.4 — Account Protection Rules

Rules to Live By

✓
Never share your login credentials with anyone — not an assistant, not a freelancer, not a course creator asking you to "show your screen"
✓
Never click suspicious links in emails or DMs claiming to be from Meta. Meta will never DM you on Instagram asking for your password or to "verify your account"
✓
Add a backup admin to your Business Suite — a trusted person who can help you recover access if you ever get locked out. Go to Business Settings → People → Add people
✓
Check your active sessions regularly — Security and Login → Where You're Logged In. Remove any devices you don't recognise
💡 If you ever get hacked
Go immediately to facebook.com/hacked — Meta's official recovery tool. Change your password, remove unrecognised devices, and report the breach. Act fast — the sooner you report it, the better.

Module 03 of 09 · Tracking

Installing the
Meta Pixel

The Pixel is a tiny piece of code that connects your website to Meta. Without it, Meta can't track who's buying, can't optimise for purchases, and can't learn who your best customers are. Install it before you spend a single dollar on ads.

3.1 — What Is the Pixel?

Why You Can't Run Ads Without It

When someone visits your website, the Pixel fires and sends information back to Meta — things like "someone viewed this page," "someone added to cart," or "someone made a purchase." Meta uses this data to find more people like your buyers and optimise who sees your ads.

💡 Simple version
The Pixel is how Meta knows your ads are working. Without it, you're driving traffic blind — you'll see link clicks but you'll never know if those clicks turned into sales.
3.2 — Create Your Pixel

Setting Up the Pixel in Business Manager

01
In Business Manager, go to Business Settings
02
Click Data Sources → Datasets (this is what Meta now calls Pixels)
03
Click Add → Create a New Dataset
04
Name it using your brand name (e.g. "AMCREATIVE Pixel") — you only ever need one Pixel per business
05
Click Create
06
Connect it to your ad account when prompted
07
Your Pixel ID is the 15–16 digit number shown in the dataset — copy and save this, you'll need it in the next step

You now have a Pixel. The next step is installing it on your website or store.

3.3 — Install the Pixel on Your Store or Website

Connecting Pixel to Where You Sell

How you install it depends on where your sales page lives. Find your platform below:

Stan Store
Go to Stan Store dashboard → Settings → Integrations → Meta Pixel → paste your Pixel ID (a 15-16 digit number from Events Manager) → save
Shopify
Go to Shopify Admin → Sales Channels → Facebook & Instagram → connect your Meta account → Shopify installs the Pixel automatically
WordPress
Install the free PixelYourSite plugin → activate it → paste your Pixel ID → save settings
Custom site
In Events Manager, click Install code manually → copy the base code → paste it into the <head> section of every page on your site
Linktree / other link pages
Go to your platform's settings and look for "Pixel" or "Tracking" — most modern link-in-bio tools support Meta Pixel natively
▶
YouTube search"how to install Meta Pixel on Stan Store 2026" or "how to install Meta Pixel 2026" — search for your specific platform to get a visual walkthrough
3.4 — Verify Your Pixel Is Working

Using Meta Pixel Helper

Before you run any ads, confirm your Pixel is actually firing. Don't assume — verify.

01
Open Google Chrome and go to the Chrome Web Store → search "Meta Pixel Helper" → install the free extension
02
Visit your website or Stan Store page in Chrome
03
Click the Pixel Helper icon in your browser toolbar (it looks like a small "</>" symbol)
04
You should see your Pixel ID listed with a green checkmark and "PageView" event — this means it's working correctly
05
If you see a red error or nothing at all — the Pixel isn't installed correctly. Go back to step 3.3 and reinstall
✓ What you're looking for
Green checkmark + your Pixel ID + "PageView" = installed and firing correctly. That's all you need to confirm before moving on.

Module 04 of 09 · Foundations

Understanding
Ads Manager

Ads Manager can look overwhelming the first time you open it. This module gives you a clear map so nothing surprises you — before you create your first campaign.

4.1 — The Three-Level Structure

Campaign → Ad Set → Ad

Every ad you run lives inside a three-level hierarchy. Understanding this is the most important thing you can learn before you start.

Campaign
Your goal. What do you want Meta to optimise for? (Sales, Traffic, Awareness, etc.) One campaign can contain multiple ad sets
Ad Set
Your who and where. This is where you define your audience, your placement (where the ad shows), and your budget. One ad set can contain multiple ads
Ad
Your creative. The actual image or video and copy that people see. This is what gets shown to your audience
💡 Think of it like this
Campaign = the mission (I want to get sales)
Ad Set = the plan (I'm going to target women 25-40 interested in digital products, spending $15/day)
Ad = the execution (here's the video and copy they'll see)
4.2 — Key Vocabulary

Words You'll See Everywhere

You don't need to memorise all of these right now — just read through so nothing looks foreign when you're in campaign setup.

Objective
The goal of your campaign. For selling products, always choose Sales
Audience
Who sees your ad — defined by location, age, gender, interests, or Meta's AI (Advantage+)
Placement
Where your ad appears — Facebook Feed, Instagram Feed, Reels, Stories, etc.
Creative
Your image or video — the visual part of your ad
Copy
The text in your ad — Primary Text (above creative) and Headline (below creative)
Budget
How much you're willing to spend — set at campaign or ad set level
Impression
One time your ad was shown to someone (same person can generate multiple impressions)
Reach
The number of unique people who saw your ad
Click
Someone clicked your ad link
Conversion
Someone completed your goal — e.g., made a purchase
Learning Phase
The period when Meta's algorithm is figuring out who's most likely to convert. Usually the first 7–14 days
4.3 — Navigating the Dashboard

Finding Your Way Around

Campaigns tab
Overview of all your campaigns — status (active/paused), budget, results. This is your home base
Ad Sets tab
Drill into audience settings, placements, and scheduling for each ad set
Ads tab
See each individual ad's performance — which creative is getting clicks, which isn't
Columns button
Customise what data shows in your table — you'll do this in the Analytics module
Filters & Date Range
Top right of the screen — use this to look at specific time periods (last 7 days, last 30 days, etc.)

Running Your

You have your accounts, your security, your Pixel, and your dashboard orientation. Now it's time to build and publish your first ad — step by step, click by click.

Creating the Campaign

01

02

03
Name your campaign clearly — use a format like: ProductName · Sales · Month/Year (e.g. "TheSetup · Sales · May2026")

5.2 — Ad Set Setup

Defining Who Sees Your Ad

01
Under Conversion event, select Purchase — this links to your Pixel and tells Meta you want sales
02
For audience: choose Advantage+ Audience — Meta's AI will find your buyers. Don't overthink targeting at this stage
03
For placements: choose Advantage+ Placements — Meta decides where to show your ad based on performance
04
Set your start date and confirm everything looks correct → click Next
⚠ Don't narrow your audience
Resist the urge to add lots of interests, age restrictions, or location filters. The more you restrict, the less room Meta has to find your actual buyers. Start broad.

5.4 — After You Publish

What to Expect in the First 72 Hours

01
Your ad enters Review — Meta checks it against their ad policies. Usually takes a few minutes, can take up to 24 hours
02
Once approved, it enters the Learning Phase — Meta's algorithm is figuring out who is most likely to buy. Performance fluctuates. This is completely normal
03
Meta needs roughly 50 purchase events per week to fully exit the learning phase
🚫 Do NOT touch your ad for at least 3–5 days
No edits. No budget changes. No pausing and restarting. Every change resets the learning phase and you lose all the data Meta has collected so far. Wait.

Module 06 of 09 · Budget

Budgets &
Spending

How much to spend, how Meta charges you, and the golden rules for scaling without blowing your budget or breaking what's already working.

6.1 — How Much Should a Beginner Spend?

Your Starting Budget

→
Start with $10–$20/day per ad set — enough signal for Meta to learn without burning through money too fast
→
Plan to spend 2–3× your product price before judging results. Selling a $17 product? Spend $34–$51 before deciding if the ad works
→
Think of early spend as learning budget — you're buying information about your audience, not just paying for sales
💡 The mindset shift
Every dollar you spend gives Meta more data about who buys from you. Even a campaign that doesn't profit in week one is teaching Meta's algorithm something valuable. Beginners who quit after $20 never get to the good part.
6.2 — Daily vs. Lifetime Budget

Which One to Use

Daily
Use this. Meta spends roughly this amount each day. Simple to control, easy to adjust
Lifetime
You set a total and Meta spreads it over a date range. Harder to predict day-to-day — come back to this once you're more experienced
6.3 — How Meta Charges You

Understanding Your Billing

01
Meta charges when you hit a billing threshold OR at the end of the month — whichever comes first
02
Check your charges anytime: Billing → Payment Activity in Ads Manager
03
Set an Account Spending Limit before you run ads — this caps your total spend and is your safety net against accidental overspending. Find it in Billing → Payment Settings
⚠ Do this right now
Set your Account Spending Limit before your ad goes live. This is the number one thing beginners forget — and then they panic when they see a charge they weren't expecting.
6.4 — Scaling Your Budget

When and How to Spend More

01
Only increase budget after 3+ days of stable, consistent results — not after one good day
02
Increase by 20–30% at a time — not doubling overnight
03
Know that every budget change resets the learning phase — expect a few days of fluctuation after each increase

📊 Starting targets

ROAS above 1.0 = you're at least breaking even (making back your ad spend)

7.4 — How to Read Data Without Panicking

The Rules for Checking Your Results

→
Check every 24–48 hours. Not every hour. Hourly data is noise — it will stress you out and mean nothing
→
Give it 3–5 days before making any changes. Day 1 is almost always the worst day of a campaign
→
Look at trends, not single days. One bad day does not mean a bad campaign. One good day does not mean you should double your budget

Module 08 of 09 · Troubleshooting

Troubleshooting &
Common Mistakes

Things will go wrong. An ad will get rejected. A campaign will underperform. This module tells you exactly what to do — and lists the mistakes most beginners make so you don't have to make them.

8.1 — Ad Rejected?

How to Fix It

Getting an ad rejected feels scary. It's almost always fixable. Here are the most common reasons:

Reason
Before/after claims — e.g. "I went from $0 to $5k." Change to: "here's what changed for me" — softer, story-based language
Reason
Personal attribute language — e.g. "Are you struggling with anxiety?" Meta flags language that implies you know personal details about the viewer. Change to: "For anyone navigating…"
Reason
Prohibited content — check the rejection email for the exact policy Meta flagged
Fix it
Edit the rejected ad → update the flagged copy or image → click Publish again
Appeal it
If you think the rejection was wrong, click Request Review — a Meta human will manually re-review your ad
▶
YouTube search"Facebook ad rejected how to fix 2026" — look for a video that walks through the most common rejection reasons and how to resubmit
8.2 — Top Beginner Mistakes

What Not to Do

✕
Editing ads during the learning phase — resets everything. Wait 3–5 days minimum
✕
Judging results after 1 day — day 1 is almost always the worst. Give it time
✕
Using Engagement or Awareness objectives when you want sales — these optimise for clicks and views, not purchases
✕
Over-targeting — stacking 10 interests, restricting age too tightly. Let Meta's AI do its job
✕
Running only one ad — always have 2–3 creative variations so you have data to compare
✕
Running ads without the Pixel — Meta can't track sales and can't optimise. Always verify the Pixel first
✕
No 2FA — you've already done this, but never remove it
8.3 — When to Turn Off an Ad

Knowing When to Pause

⚠ Wait for enough data first
Pausing too early is one of the most expensive beginner mistakes. You need sufficient spend (at least 2–3× your product price) before you can make a valid decision.
Pause if
CPA is 3× your target after adequate spend
Pause if
CTR is below 0.5% after 1,000+ impressions — the creative isn't working
Pause if
ROAS is below 1.0 after sufficient data — you're losing money on every sale
Don't pause
After one bad day. After less than $30 spent. Because you're nervous

💡 The iteration mindset
Your first campaign is never your best campaign. Its job is to give you data. Every campaign after that is an improvement. The people who succeed with ads are the ones who keep going — not the ones who get it right on attempt one.
